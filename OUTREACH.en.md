## Abstract

This project brings together the official log of Ingenuity's 72 flights, the NASA/JPL-Caltech map, a chronological montage of public imagery, exploratory visual metrics and Perseverance SuperCam recordings associated with flights 4, 5, 6 and 8. It compares the post-processed flight 9 hazard-avoidance demonstration, examines a landing near a rock and independently reproduces rotor acoustic signatures. The JPL test's harmonic comb gives 2,276.44 rpm, compared with the displayed 2,277 rpm; the paper provides an equivalent structure from a separate recording. The project also includes a protocol for testing the author's published 31–32 ridges hypothesis and an evidence-graded review of the loading history of flights 62, 68 and 69 at 10 m/s. Claims are distinguished as observed, reproduced or hypothetical. This is an independent, traceable and extensible technical record based on public material, not internal telemetry or a NASA/JPL-endorsed product.

## Supported outreach statements

- Public sources and original analysis share one chronology.
- The descriptive decline in final-sequence visual references is consistent with difficult terrain, without establishing causality.
- Flight 9 hazard avoidance shares a conceptual need for visible structure; its algorithm differs from this metric.
- Equal WAV durations, sampling rates and formats explain equal file sizes, not identical contents.
- Flight 5 modulation implies order-1-rpm ΔRPM under the four-lobe model; it is not telemetry or a demonstrated fault.
- The ridge hypothesis began with 31 reviewed images and now has a test protocol.
- NASA documents 10 m/s on flights 62/68/69 and Sys-ID on 68–69; fatigue contribution to flight 72 remains unproven.

## Claims to avoid

Do not claim reproduction of Ingenuity's navigation algorithm, NASA confirmation of the 32-ridge theory, autonomous selection of each ridge, ΔRPM or fast flights causing blade failure, the visual score being an internal flight variable, official endorsement or unpublished telemetry.
